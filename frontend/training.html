Our Training Philosophy

Positive reinforcement is at the heart of effective dog training

1

Patience

Every dog learns at their own pace. Stay calm and consistent.

2

Reward

Use treats, praise, and play to reinforce desired behaviors.

3

Consistency

Use the same commands and rules every time for clarity.

4

Practice

Short, frequent sessions are more effective than long ones.

Basic Commands

These foundational commands create the building blocks for all future training

1
Sit Easy

Hold a treat above your dog's nose, move it back over their head. As they look up, their bottom naturally lowers. Say "sit" and reward immediately.

2
Stay Medium

Start with your dog in "sit". Hold your palm out and say "stay". Take one step back, then return and reward. Gradually increase distance and duration.

3
Come Medium

Use a happy voice and say "come" while backing away. Reward enthusiastically when they reach you. Practice in safe, enclosed areas first.

4
Down Medium

From "sit", hold a treat to their nose, then slowly lower it to the ground. As they follow it down, say "down" and reward when elbows touch floor.

5
Heel Advanced

Keep your dog at your left side while walking. Use treats to lure them into position. Say "heel" and reward for staying beside you without pulling.

6
Leave It Medium

Hold a treat in your closed fist. When your dog stops trying to get it, say "leave it" and give them a different treat. Essential for safety.

House Training

Establish good habits from day one with these proven techniques

🕐 Establish a Schedule

Take your dog outside first thing in the morning, after meals, after naps, and before bed. Puppies need to go out every 2-3 hours. Consistency is key - use the same door and the same spot in your yard.

🎉 Celebrate Success

When your dog goes potty outside, praise them enthusiastically and give a treat immediately. The reward must come right after the behavior so they understand what they're being praised for.

🏠 Crate Training

Dogs naturally avoid soiling their sleeping area. A properly-sized crate (big enough to stand, turn, lie down) helps with house training. Never use it as punishment - it should be a safe, comfortable den.

👀 Watch for Signs

Learn to recognize when your dog needs to go: sniffing the floor, circling, whining, or heading toward the door. Interrupt accidents with a gentle "oops!" and immediately take them outside.

🧹 Clean Accidents Properly

Use an enzymatic cleaner to eliminate odors completely. Dogs are drawn to spots where they've gone before. Regular cleaners may seem effective to us but dogs can still detect the scent.

⏳ Be Patient

Most puppies aren't fully house trained until 4-6 months old. Some breeds take longer. Never punish accidents - it only teaches your dog to hide when they need to go, making training harder.

Socialization

The critical period for socialization is 3-14 weeks, but it's never too late to start

👥 People Exposure

Introduce your dog to people of all ages, sizes, and appearances. Include people wearing hats, sunglasses, uniforms, and using wheelchairs or walkers. Make each encounter positive with treats and calm praise.

🐕 Other Dogs

Start with calm, well-socialized dogs. Puppy classes are excellent for controlled introductions. Watch body language - loose, wiggly bodies are good; stiff postures or prolonged staring are warning signs.

🌍 New Environments

Expose your dog to different surfaces (grass, gravel, metal grates), sounds (traffic, construction, appliances), and places (parks, pet stores, outdoor cafes). Go at their pace.

🚗 Car Travel

Start with short trips to fun destinations. Use a secure crate or safety harness. Never leave dogs in parked cars. Associate the car with positive experiences, not just vet visits.

✋ Handling Exercises

Get your dog comfortable with having their ears, paws, mouth, and tail touched. This makes vet visits and grooming much easier. Pair handling with treats and keep sessions short.

😰 Signs of Stress

Watch for tucked tail, ears back, lip licking, yawning, or trying to escape. If your dog is stressed, increase distance from the trigger and go slower. Never force interactions.

Behavior Correction

Address common behavioral issues with positive, effective methods

🔊 Excessive Barking

Identify the trigger (boredom, alert, attention-seeking). Don't yell - it sounds like you're barking too! Teach "quiet" by waiting for silence, marking it, and rewarding. Address the underlying cause.

⬆️ Jumping on People

Turn away and ignore your dog when they jump. Only give attention when all four paws are on the ground. Teach an incompatible behavior like "sit to greet." Consistency from all family members is crucial.

🦴 Destructive Chewing

Provide plenty of appropriate chew toys. Puppy-proof your space. When you catch them chewing something wrong, redirect to an appropriate item and praise. More exercise often reduces destructive behavior.

🍽️ Food Guarding

Approach while your dog eats and drop high-value treats in their bowl. This teaches that people approaching = good things. Never punish guarding - it makes it worse. Consult a professional for severe cases.

😟 Separation Anxiety

Build independence gradually. Practice short absences and slowly increase duration. Don't make departures emotional. Provide enrichment toys when alone. Severe cases may need professional help and possibly medication.

🏃 Counter Surfing

Management is key - don't leave food unattended. Teach "off" and "leave it." Reward four paws on the floor. Never let them succeed at getting food from counters, as intermittent rewards make behaviors harder to stop.

Leash Training

Enjoy walks together with proper leash manners

🎽 Equipment Matters

Use a front-clip harness or head halter for pullers. Avoid retractable leashes during training. A 6-foot leash gives enough freedom while maintaining control. Ensure collar/harness fits properly.

🏠 Start Indoors

Practice leash skills in your house or yard first where there are fewer distractions. Get your dog comfortable wearing the leash before adding the challenge of new environments.

🛑 Be a Tree

When your dog pulls, stop moving completely. Wait for slack in the leash, then continue walking. Your dog learns that pulling = stopping, while loose leash = forward movement.

↩️ Change Direction

When your dog pulls ahead, do a u-turn and walk the other way. This teaches them to pay attention to where you're going. Reward them for checking in with you during walks.

🏆 Reward Position

Carry treats and reward your dog frequently for walking beside you. Start with continuous rewards, then gradually space them out. Mark and treat the moment they're in the right position.

🐕 Sniff Breaks

Walks are enriching for dogs - let them sniff! Use "go sniff" as a reward for good walking. Balance training time with free exploration time. A tired nose is a tired dog.
